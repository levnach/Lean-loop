## Writing rules (keep it simple)
- Never explain what Z3 does, what Lean is, what "unsat" means, or why the project exists. Start directly with what this PR changed.
- Use the standard terms of logic and SMT solving: "uninterpreted function", "sort", "Boolean", "if-then-else term" (written `ite` in SMT-LIB), "literal", "clause", "congruence", "refutation", and so on. Never replace a standard term with an invented everyday phrase such as "unknown function" or "kind of value".
- Where terms are explained: the plain-words definitions live in the Glossary, the last section before the footer. The header, "In one paragraph", and "Background" contain no definitions at all; there, wrap the first occurrence of each standard term in a link to its Glossary entry (`<a href="#g-sort">sort</a>`). From "What changed" onward you may add a short plain-words gloss on a term's first use in that section when the sentence would otherwise be unclear, but prefer the link. Never define the same term twice in the running text.
- The simplicity is in the sentences and the explanations, not in avoiding the vocabulary. A reader should finish the page knowing the correct words for what changed.
- Short sentences, about 15 to 20 words. One idea per sentence. No semicolons, no nested parentheses.
- Use a short analogy where it helps a reader grasp a specific change.
- Name internal identifiers (file names, function names, Lean binders, hint kinds, Z3 component names) only when the reader needs them to find something. Never more than one per sentence. Put them in <code>.
- Keep the "What changed" section to three to six bullets, one plain-language sentence each saying what a reader can now do that they could not before. Put the file table after the bullets.
- In "How it was verified", lead with the plain meaning ("about 600 random puzzles all passed") and keep exact counts in the table.
- In "Review", describe each finding as a story: what the reviewer tried, what went wrong, how it was fixed. Explain why it mattered.
- End with a "Glossary" section, the last section before the footer, one entry per term in alphabetical order, each with an id the links above point to (`<dt id="g-sort">sort</dt><dd>The type of a value, such as Int, Real, or Bool.</dd>`). It is the one place where terms are explained: list every standard term the page uses (uninterpreted function, sort, congruence, cut, Farkas lemma, tactic, and so on) with a one-line plain-words definition, and a short example where it helps. Do not include Z3, Lean, unsat, or the project goal.
- Numbers go in tables, not in prose, unless a single number is the point.
